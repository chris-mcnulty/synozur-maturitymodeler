Create “Synozur Information Security: Annual Training” as a draft, tenant-private Orion course.

The learner should leave knowing how to recognize suspicious requests, protect accounts and information, and report problems promptly.

R — Role

Act as a security-awareness instructional designer. Teach practical decisions without fear-based language or unnecessary technical detail.

A — Audience

All Synozur staff, including nontechnical employees. Distinguish general awareness from additional training required for administrators, developers, and incident responders.

M — Method

Course sequence
Welcome and responsibilities

Introductory video placeholder and transcript.
Objectives and a short explanation of confidentiality, integrity, and availability.
Explain that course completion supports readiness but does not certify the organization.
Accounts and suspicious requests

Password managers, individual identities, MFA, and least privilege.
Phishing by email, chat, QR code, phone, or impersonated video.
Independent verification using a known contact channel.
Safe tools, devices, and sharing

Approved devices, updates, remote work, physical security, and lost equipment.
Approved AI tools and account contexts.
Client sharing restrictions and why personal storage is not a workaround.
Additional handling review for suspected CUI.
Reporting and practice

What to report, what facts to preserve, and what not to investigate.
Observable insider-risk behaviors without accusations or profiling.
A concise downloadable “What to do first” reference with verified reporting details.
Knowledge check and attestation

Native quiz, feedback, and separate typed-name acknowledgement.
Practice situations
Develop the source’s urgent IT-support call into a decision exercise. The caller knows the project name, requests an MFA approval, and asks for remote-access software installation.

Add two fictional situations:

A delivery deadline tempts someone to bypass a blocked client-sharing route.
A staff member enters credentials on a suspicious page and must decide what to do next.
Use native questions and feedback. Do not build a new branching engine.

Introduction video
Use this script for Chris’s recording:

Hi, I’m Chris McNulty, CTO at Synozur. Every one of us handles information that clients and colleagues depend on us to protect.

This course covers the decisions we make during an ordinary workday: approving a sign-in, opening a message, sharing a file, or trying a new AI tool.

Some attacks look convincing. A caller may know your name and your project. A message may arrive at exactly the moment you’re expecting a document. Take the time to verify a request through a channel you trust.

If you make a mistake or notice something unusual, report it promptly. You don’t need to prove an incident happened before asking for help.

Work through the examples, complete the review questions, and read the attestation before signing. If anything is unclear, raise it before you acknowledge.

E — Execution

Build the course under the shared acceptance criteria. Keep urgent and after-hours reporting details as release blockers until verified. Preserve the distinction between the published IT support address and a confirmed incident-response channel.
