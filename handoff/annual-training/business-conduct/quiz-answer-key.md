# Quiz answer and rationale sheet (administrator only)

Orion stores one explanation per question and shows it after each submission, whether the answer was right or wrong. It doesn’t show per-choice feedback. The per-choice feedback below is for facilitators and reviewers; learners see the “Learner sees after submitting” text. Answer keys and explanations are removed from learner course data and graded on the server.

## 2.3 Practice: A favor during selection

Practice, not graded (passing score 0).

### 1. Which details matter when assessing whether the selection is impartial?

| | Choice | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A | Only the approximate value of the trip; a friendship is private. | The relationship and timing are relevant to a perceived conflict. |
| B ✓ | The relationship, offer, timing, secrecy request, and any relevant messages. | These details give context for an impartial assessment. |
| C | Only the bidder's price; personal ties cannot affect a selection. | Price alone does not address the possible effect of a personal tie. |
| D | An account that omits the secrecy request to avoid speculation. | Omitting secrecy would conceal a material fact. |

- **Learner sees after submitting:** Value alone misses the relationship and timing; price alone misses the personal tie. Omitting the secrecy request distorts the facts needed to assess impartiality. Revisit “Disclose conflicts before acting.”

## 5.1 Final knowledge check

### 1. A relative owns a supplier you are evaluating. What should you do?

| | Choice | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A ✓ | Disclose the relationship before participating and follow the mitigation decision. | Early disclosure permits impartial review. |
| B | Participate without disclosure if the price is good. | Price does not erase a conflict. |

- **Source:** Deck slide 12 (SYN-CON-2026-Q01); notes: A

### 2. A vendor offers an expensive trip during a live selection and asks for secrecy. What is best?

| | Choice | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A | Accept because no money changes hands. | Noncash travel can influence a decision. |
| B | Accept if it happens outside work hours. | Outside hours do not remove a selection conflict. |
| C | Split the expense into small entries. | Splitting hides the true cost. |
| D ✓ | Decline or pause and report it for review. | Review addresses timing, influence, and secrecy. |

- **Correct:** D
- **Learner sees after submitting:** Travel can be improper influence without cash or work hours; splitting an expense conceals it. Secrecy during a live selection calls for review. Revisit “Do not trade favors for business.”
- **Revisit after an incorrect response:** Do not trade favors for business
- **Source:** Deck slide 13 (SYN-CON-2026-Q02); notes: D
